Before you begin

What is about to happen, in one paragraph, so that nothing below is a surprise.

Where things stand today. Your web address, workingwithgod.live, is registered at GoDaddy. Right now it points at the old GoDaddy website builder — GoDaddy calls this product Websites + Marketing on your bill and Airo on some newer screens, and you pay a subscription for it either way. Your email, eliyahu@workingwithgod.live, runs on Microsoft and is completely separate from the website, even though both are managed on the same GoDaddy page.

Where we are going. The new site is built and already live on Netlify at a temporary address. We point workingwithgod.live at Netlify instead of at the GoDaddy builder, confirm the new site is answering, and only then cancel that old subscription.

A few words this guide uses

Six words come up again and again below. Read this once and the rest of the guide needs no further translation.

WordWhat it actually means
Domain Your web address, workingwithgod.live. You already own it, and nothing in this guide changes that.
DNS The internet’s phone book. It is what turns the address workingwithgod.live, which a person types, into the location of the actual computer serving your website, which is all a browser understands.
DNS record One entry in that phone book: “for this name, go to this place.” Part 2 changes three of these and leaves the rest exactly as they are.
A record / CNAME record The two kinds of entry involved here. An A record points a name straight at a number, like 75.2.60.5. A CNAME record points a name at another name instead. Both do the same job of saying where to go.
TTL “Time To Live.” How long the rest of the internet is allowed to remember an old answer before it checks again. Turning this down first is what makes the switch fast rather than slow.
Nameservers A bigger, separate setting that controls your entire phone book at once, not one entry in it. This guide never touches these — see the warning below.

What you need

  • A computer with a web browser. A phone will not work for this.
  • Your GoDaddy sign-in, and your Netlify sign-in.
  • About 20 minutes of clicking, plus a one-hour wait in the middle of Part 2 that you can spend doing something else.
Your website never goes dark, and your email is never at risk

Nothing here switches a website off. Both sites — old and new — stay running while the address moves between them, so every visitor lands on a working site at every moment. Part 2 explains this properly.

Your email is delivered by a separate set of rows on the GoDaddy page. We change three rows and leave the rest alone. Mail keeps flowing throughout.

You cannot break anything, but do not guess

Both GoDaddy and Netlify rename their own buttons every few months — sometimes a menu is called one thing on one screen and a slightly different thing on another, even inside the same company’s website. Where that happens, this guide says so. Anywhere else, if a screen truly does not resemble the description here, stop and send us a screenshot rather than guessing. Guessing on the DNS page in Part 2 is the one place in this whole project where a wrong click has a real cost — everywhere else, the worst case is simply redoing a step.

Part 01

Quick check: is the new site ready?

Your forms, your notification emails and your Google spreadsheet already work — full detail on all of that is in the other guide we sent you. This part is a two-minute look at the new site, on its temporary address, before Part 2 switches your real address over to it.

About 2 minutes
  1. 01

    Sign in at app.netlify.com. You will see one entry listed, workingwithgod. Click it.

    Near the top is a temporary web address, workingwithgod.netlify.app. Click it and have a look around. It is a fully working copy of the new site — it is simply not on your own address yet. That is what Part 2 fixes.

    One thing that can look odd. Netlify is part-way through renaming things, so the same website is called a site on some screens and a project on others. They mean the same thing, and you only have one of them.

  2. 02

    If you would like to see the whole chain working end to end, fill in one form on that temporary address yourself — the Roundtable one is a good choice — using your own name and email address.

    Within a minute or two you should see the notification email arrive, and a new row appear on the Roundtable tab of your spreadsheet, exactly as described in the other guide. If both of those happen, everything is ready and this part is done.

    This step is optional. If you already know the forms are working — you have tested them recently, or real registrations are already arriving — skip straight to Part 2.

Nothing to set up here

This part is a check, not a setup step. Forms, notification emails and your spreadsheet work exactly the same whether a visitor reaches the site through workingwithgod.netlify.app, as they do today, or through workingwithgod.live, as they will after Part 2. Pointing your address at the new site does not change any of that.

Part 02

Pointing workingwithgod.live at the new site

Your web address currently points at the old GoDaddy site. This changes where it points. It is the only step in this guide that carries any real risk, so read the warning first.

About 10 minutes, then a wait
Your site does not go down while you do this

The new site is already built and already live — it is sitting at workingwithgod.netlify.app right now, serving pages. The old site is still live too. What follows does not switch anything off. It only changes which of the two your web address points at.

The moment a visitor’s computer picks up the new answer, it lands on the new site. Before that moment, it lands on the old one. Either way it lands on a working website. There is no window where nothing is there.

Three things keep that handover short, and all three are built into the steps below: add the address to Netlify before you touch GoDaddy, lower the TTL an hour ahead (step 2), and cancel the old subscription last, not first.

Your email lives at this same address

Mail to eliyahu@workingwithgod.live is delivered by a handful of settings sitting on this same page. We change three rows and leave everything else exactly as it is, so your email keeps working throughout. Nothing below will interrupt it.

Two things to say no to. If GoDaddy offers to change your nameservers, or to connect your domain to another service for you, decline both and tell us. Those hand the whole page over at once, and your email goes with it.

The two things to have open. GoDaddy, on the DNS page for workingwithgod.live. And Netlify, on Domain management, where you add workingwithgod.live and www.workingwithgod.live to the project first, using whichever button offers to add a domain you already own.

Somewhere in that process Netlify asks how the address’s DNS will be managed. Choose the option that means “I will manage this myself, elsewhere” — worded along the lines of external DNS provider or third-party DNS. The exact wording shifts from time to time; any option that is not “set up Netlify DNS” is the right one, because your domain stays at GoDaddy throughout.

  1. 01

    Sign in at GoDaddy. Find workingwithgod.live in your products, then open DNS or Manage DNS.

    You will see a table of rows with columns like Type, Name and Value. This is the address book that tells the internet where to find your things.

  2. 02

    Optional, but it makes the switchover about six times quicker. An hour before you do the rest, edit the A rows and the www row and set each one's TTL to Custom › 600 seconds. Save, then leave it for an hour and come back.

    TTL is how long the rest of the internet is allowed to remember the old answer. Lowering it first shrinks the gap where the site is half-switched from about an hour to about ten minutes. Change nothing else while you are in there.

  3. 03

    This step points the plain address, workingwithgod.live, at the new site.

    You currently have two rows of type A named @. Both belong to the old website. Delete them both, then add one new row:

    Delete these two A   @   13.248.243.5
    A   @   76.223.105.230
    Add this one Type:   A
    Name:   @
    Value:  75.2.60.5
    TTL:    600

    The @ means the address on its own, with no www in front of it.

    If GoDaddy will not let you edit the A record

    The old website builder may still be attached to the domain and holding it. In your GoDaddy products, open the old website builder (Websites + Marketing, sometimes shown as Airo) and disconnect the domain from it. The DNS rows then become editable.

  4. 04

    This step points www.workingwithgod.live at the new site.

    You already have a row of type CNAME named www. Edit that row. Change only its Value:

    Edit the row you already have Type:   CNAME   (leave as it is)
    Name:   www     (leave as it is)
    Value:  workingwithgod.netlify.app  ← change to this
    TTL:    600
    Do not add an A record named www

    Several tutorials online tell you to add a new row of type A named www. If you try, GoDaddy will refuse with “Record name www conflicts with another record”.

    GoDaddy is right to refuse. A name can be a CNAME or an A, never both, and yours is already a CNAME. Nothing needs adding here — you are editing the row that already exists.

  5. 05

    Save. Change nothing else.

    These rows are your email and your Microsoft account. Leave every one of them exactly as it is, and do not delete any row you do not recognise:

    Do not touch MX                      (mail delivery)
    TXT                   (both of them — SPF and Microsoft)
    autodiscover      (Outlook set-up)
    msoid, email      (Microsoft sign-in)
    _domainconnect    (GoDaddy’s own automation)
    _sip… (two rows) (Teams)

    Also add no row of type AAAA. Netlify does not serve the address you are pointing at over that kind of record, and adding one breaks the site.

  6. 06

    Wait. If you lowered the TTL an hour beforehand, most visitors see the new site within about ten minutes, and everyone has it within the hour. If you skipped that step, allow up to an hour for most and a few hours for the last stragglers.

    You do not have to sit and watch. Open workingwithgod.live in a private or incognito window every few minutes; the moment the new site appears there, it has switched. Netlify’s Domain management page also turns green on its own once it sees the change.

    For the first few minutes after it switches, the address may show a security warning about its certificate. Netlify issues the certificate automatically as soon as it can see the domain pointing at it, and the warning clears itself. Nothing is broken and there is nothing to do.

What changed, at a glance A    @    13.248.243.5    ← deleted (old site)
A    @    76.223.105.230    ← deleted (old site)
A    @    75.2.60.5    ← added (new site)
CNAME    www    workingwithgod.netlify.app    ← edited, same row you already had

Everything else on the page — MX, TXT, autodiscover, msoid, email, _domainconnect, the two _sip rows — is untouched.

Leave the old website alone for now

It costs nothing to let it keep running for another few days, and while it is running you have something to fall back on. Part 3 is the point at which you cancel it, and only after four checks have passed.

Part 03

Retiring the old GoDaddy website

Only once the new site is answering at your own address. Doing this first is the one way to actually take your website offline, so the order here matters more than the steps do.

About 5 minutes
Do not cancel anything until all four of these are true

Work down the list. If any one of them is not true yet, wait — there is no deadline here, and the old subscription costing you one more month is far cheaper than a website that is down.

  1. 01

    Open a private or incognito browser window and go to workingwithgod.live. The new site loads.

    A private window matters. A normal one may still be showing you a saved copy of the old site, which tells you nothing.

  2. 02

    In the same window, go to www.workingwithgod.live. The new site loads there too, with no security warning.

  3. 03

    Send yourself an email from any other address to eliyahu@workingwithgod.live, and confirm it arrives. Then send one from that address to yourself, and confirm that arrives too.

    Both directions. Receiving works through one set of rows and sending through another, so testing only one direction proves only half of it.

  4. 04

    Register on the new site using your own details, and confirm the email reaches you.

  5. 05

    Now, and only now, find the old website builder. Sign in at account.godaddy.com/products (or click My Products from the menu at the top of any GoDaddy page), and look for Websites + Marketing — on some accounts this is labelled Airo instead. Either name means the same subscription.

    Cancel the website. Do not cancel the domain or the email.

    All three sit on the same products page, and it is easy to tick the wrong one.

    On the GoDaddy products page Websites + Marketing (or Airo)  ← cancel this one
    Domain: workingwithgod.live  ← KEEP
    Microsoft 365 / Email     ← KEEP

    Cancelling the domain would hand your web address back to the world. Cancelling the email would stop your mail. Neither is recoverable in a hurry. You are only touching the website-builder line.

  6. 06

    Cancelling is a few small screens rather than one click, and there is a final email you must open before it actually finishes. None of the individual steps are difficult, but missing the last one is the single easiest way to think you are done when you are not:

    The screens, in order 1. Next to Websites + Marketing (or Airo), choose Turn off Auto-Renew
       (if you see a plain Delete button instead, auto-renew was
       already off — select that and skip to step 4 below)
    2. Pick any reason from the list GoDaddy offers, then Continue to Cancel
    3. Tick the box that says you understand, then select Send email
    4. Open the email GoDaddy sends you and select Yes, I Want to Delete!
    5. Back on the GoDaddy page, a final Delete product button confirms it

    That confirmation email is not spam and not optional — GoDaddy sends it on purpose, as a safety net so a subscription cannot be cancelled by mistake with one accidental click. If it has not arrived within a few minutes, check your spam folder before trying again.

  7. 07

    Along the way GoDaddy may ask why you are leaving, or offer a discount to stay. Answer however you like and decline — it does not affect anything below.

    If a screen warns that “your website will no longer be available”, that is correct and expected. It means the old site. Your new site is somewhere else entirely and is unaffected — that is exactly what you confirmed in steps 1 and 2 above.

That is the whole move

Your address now points at the new site, your email is untouched, and your GoDaddy bill is smaller by the price of a website builder you no longer need.

Reference

If something looks wrong

Everything on this list is either normal, or has a one-line fix. None of it means the site is lost.

During the switchover

What you seeWhat it means and what to do
The old site still loads at workingwithgod.live Normal for the first minutes. Your computer is still remembering the old answer. Try a private window. If it is still the old site an hour later, check the rows you edited at GoDaddy against Part 2, steps 3 and 4.
A security or certificate warning Normal in the first minutes after the change. Netlify issues the certificate automatically once it can see the address pointing at it, and the warning clears itself. Nothing to do.
The plain address works but www. does not, or the other way round Only one of the two rows was changed. Go back to Part 2 and check both step 3 (the A row) and step 4 (the www row).
GoDaddy says “Record name www conflicts with another record” You are trying to add a row where you should be editing one. Part 2, step 4 explains it. Cancel out of the add screen and edit the existing www row instead.
GoDaddy will not let you edit the A row at all The old GoDaddy website is still holding the domain. Open it in your products (Websites + Marketing or Airo) and disconnect the domain from it, then come back.
Netlify still shows the domain as not configured or with a warning It checks on a timer. Give it fifteen minutes, then use the Retry DNS verification link on the Domain management page.
A form on the new site says nothing happened, or you never got the notification email This is not a deployment problem — forms and email are covered separately in the other guide we sent you (Website Setup Guide), which has its own troubleshooting section.
Your email stopped arriving Stop, and contact us immediately. Do not change anything else at GoDaddy in the meantime. This is recoverable, and it is recoverable fastest if nothing else has been touched since.
